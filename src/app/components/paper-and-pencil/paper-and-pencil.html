TDODJ with Paper & Pencil

Take the adventure away from the screen! Everything in the digital game translates directly to a tabletop experience — grab some dice, a sheet of grid paper, and a pencil.

What You Need

  • Dice: 1d4, 1d6, 1d8, 1d10, 1d12 (a full polyhedral set works great)
  • Grid paper — each square is one dungeon square
  • Pencils and an eraser (HP and AE change a lot)
  • One character sheet per player — see the template at the bottom of this page
  • Index cards (optional) — great for tracking monsters and items

Drawing the Dungeon

One player acts as the Dungeon Keeper (DK). The DK draws the map on grid paper — walls are solid lines between squares, doors are breaks in the wall, and empty squares are the rooms and corridors.

  • Each grid square is one movement square (costs 1 AE to enter; diagonal costs 2 AE)
  • Draw walls as solid lines on the edges between squares
  • Mark doors with a small symbol: □ closed, Δ locked, ? hidden
  • Mark treshers with a circle ○
  • Mark monster starting positions with a triangle ▲
  • Mark traps with an X ✕ (only the DK knows where they are)
  • Mark the exit square with a star ★

Creating a PC

Each player fills out a character sheet. Follow these steps:

  1. Choose a Name
  2. Choose a Species — this sets your Range of Sight and Action Economy
  3. Choose a Class — this sets your HP, AC bonus, and Magic Power
  4. Roll Strength using the formula for your species
  5. Roll or assign Stamina (1d6), Mind (1d6)
  6. Calculate Max HP and starting AC using your class

Starting Stats

  • Max HP — roll based on class (above); write it down
  • Current HP — starts equal to Max HP
  • AC — your starting AC. Fighter: 2 + Str÷2 (max +5) + 1d6÷2; all other classes: 1. Armor treshers and armor items add on top in-game.
  • AE — 5 (Human/Elph) or 4 (DwarPh/Shorties)
  • Stamina — roll 1d6; adds to attack rolls
  • Mind — roll 1d6; used for spells, traps, lock-picking, and magic resistance (Mind ÷ 2, rounded down)
  • Magic Power (MP) — from class; used to fuel spells
  • NOA — Number of Attacks per turn; starts at 1
  • NOD — Number of Defends per turn; starts at 1
  • SP — Skill Points; starts at 0; awarded for kills and dungeon completion

Turn Structure

Play proceeds in turns. The Dungeon Keeper handles all monsters.

  1. Player Turn — each PC gets their full AE; spend AE on actions below
  2. Monster Turn — the DK moves and attacks with each monster (closest first)

Movement

  • Move one square in any orthogonal direction for 1 AE
  • Move diagonally for 2 AE
  • You cannot pass through walls or closed doors
  • You can enter a square occupied by a monster only to push through (DK discretion)
  • On the map, track your facing direction — you can only attack the monster you are facing (or within weapon range)

Combat

Attacking a Monster

  1. Roll d12 + weapon to-hit bonus + Stamina
  2. If the result ≥ the monster's AC, the attack hits
  3. On a hit, roll weapon damage die + Strength and subtract from the monster's HP (minimum 1)

Combo Bonus: hitting the same monster on consecutive attacks in a turn (or across turns) adds up to +4 to your hit roll.

Boost Dice

Any time a to-hit roll (PC attack, monster attack, or spell) lands on the maximum value of its die, a boost die is immediately added to the total. That boost die can itself trigger another if it also rolls maximum, creating a chain:

The chain stops as soon as a die rolls below its maximum.

Boost Attack (Fighter only)

  • Must be the first action of your turn
  • Costs all remaining AE — your turn ends immediately after
  • Add a bonus die to your hit roll: +1d3 at lower levels, increasing to +1d6 at high levels
  • Your AC is reduced by −4 until your next turn

Defending

  • A monster attacks: DK rolls d12 + monster to-hit bonus vs your AC + Stamina
  • If the roll ≥ your total, you take the monster's damage
  • Use the Defend action (1 AE) anytime on your turn to add +2 AC until your next turn — defenders become much harder to hit
  • You can Defend up to NOD times per turn; each stacks
  • AC summary: 10 + class bonus + armor items − any Boost Attack penalty + (2 × defensive actions used)

Traps

Traps are hidden until a Search roll reveals them.

  • Detect: d12 + Mind ≥ trap's Detect DC
  • Disarm: d12 + Mind ≥ trap's Disarm DC (once detected)
  • A failed lock-pick on a trapped door triggers the trap

Trap effects (DK sets amount):

  • HP damage — reduce current HP by the trap value
  • Stamina drain — lower Stamina by the trap value
  • Mind drain — lower Mind by the trap value
  • AE drain — lose that many AE from remaining actions this turn
  • Range of Sight reduction — narrow how far you can see

Treshers and Loot

When a player steps onto a tresher square the DK reveals its contents (index card works great).

  • Coins: record the type (gold > silver > copper > zinc) on your sheet
  • Weapons: note the to-hit bonus, damage die, and range; equip in hand slots
  • Armor: note the AC bonus; equip to body slot — stacks with class AC
  • Rings / Necklaces: note passive effects; equip to their slots
  • Magic Gems: add gem's value to your Magic Power when equipped
  • Potions: note healing amount; drink for 1 AE (your turn only)
  • Spells: note the spell's TN, AE cost, range, and damage

A tresher with a ✕ mark is trapped — Search before taking it.

Magic

Casting a Spell

  1. Choose a spell in your inventory; check its AE cost and range
  2. Spend the AE cost
  3. Roll d12 + Mind vs the spell's Target Number + monster Magic Resistance
  4. On success: apply the spell's damage or effect to the target
  5. On failure: spell fizzles — AE is still spent

Magic Resistance

  • Your resistance: Mind ÷ 2 (round down) — monsters must beat this when casting at you
  • Monster resistance: set by the DK on the monster card; adds to the spell DC

Monsters

The DK creates a small index card for each monster with: Name, HP, AC, to-hit bonus, damage, Magic Resistance, SP reward.

Monster behaviour

BehaviourRule
StationaryDoes not move until a PC enters its trigger zone (typically 5 squares + line of sight)
RoamingWanders each monster turn even when no PC is near
FleeingWhen HP drops below a set threshold the monster runs away
Calls reinforcementsWhen the monster spots a PC, it alerts other nearby monsters to join in

On the monster's turn: move 1 square toward the nearest PC (or 2 if roaming aimlessly), then attack if adjacent (or within weapon range). Roll d12 + to-hit vs PC's AC + Stamina.

Skill Points and Advancement

  • Earn SP for every monster killed (amount set by DK on each monster card)
  • Earn a bonus SP for completing a dungeon run
  • Between adventures spend SP 1-for-1 to permanently raise any stat by 1:

Tips for the Dungeon Keeper

  • Set ACs between 10–18 for interesting combat; monsters rarely need AC over 18.
  • Give early monsters low SP rewards (1–3) and low HP (4–12) to ease new players in.
  • Treshers with good loot are more rewarding early; powerful items mid-dungeon keep things exciting.
  • Mark trap squares with a light pencil dot players cannot see — erase when detected.
  • Monsters with Calls Reinforcements make quiet rooms into sudden chaos — use sparingly.
  • A hidden door to a secret loot room is always a welcome surprise.
