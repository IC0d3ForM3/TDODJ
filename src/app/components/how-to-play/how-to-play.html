How to Play

Explore dungeons from a first-person view, fight monsters, collect loot, and find the exit. Each run uses your Player Character (PC) — your stats, species, and class all shape how the adventure plays out.

Version 0.02: dungeons are no longer limited to one fixed layout style. You may play official dungeons, community dungeons, or imported creator-made maps that have been stocked with monsters, treasure, obstacles, notes, and traps.

Creating a PC

Head to the Dashboard → PC tab and click Create New PC. Pick a name, a species, and a class. Stats are rolled automatically using a d12 — you can reroll by changing your species or type.

PC Stats at a Glance

  • HP — Health points. Reach 0 and the run is over.
  • AC — Armor Class. Set during character creation by your class. Armor treshers and armor items add on top. Higher AC means monsters hit you less often.
  • AE — Action Economy. How many actions you have each turn (5 for Human/Elph, 4 for DwarPh/Shorties).
  • Strength — Adds to weapon damage.
  • Stamina — Adds to your attack roll.
  • Mind — Used for trap detection, lock picking, finding hidden doors, and casting spells. Also determines your Magic Resistance (Mind ÷ 2, rounded down).
  • Magic Power (MP) — How many magic points you have available to fuel spells. Casting a spell costs 1 AE and also spends that spell's Magic Cost from your MP pool.
  • Agility — Improves ranged to-hit rolls and enhances Thieph sneak attacks.
  • Range of View — How far you can see in the first-person view.

Starting a Game

Go to the Dashboard → Available Games tab. Pick a dungeon, select the PC you want to play, and hit Start. The game saves automatically as you play, so you can pick it back up any time from Active Games.

Dungeons may be official story runs, private friend-shared runs, or public community maps. Layout size and room shape can vary, but the rules of movement, combat, loot, traps, and exits stay the same.

Moving Around

The game shows a first-person view on the left and a 10 × 10 mini-map to the right. The map shows your position and everything within your range of view.

Keyboard shortcuts

  • Arrow Keys — turn to face that direction.
  • Space or F — move forward one square.
  • B — move backward one square.
  • A — attack an adjacent monster.

You can also use the on-screen direction pad beneath the mini-map. Tap the outer buttons to face a direction (including diagonals) and F in the centre to step forward. Moving diagonally costs 2 AE instead of 1.

Action Bar Icons

A vertical icon bar sits to the right of the first-person view. These are quick-action buttons for the most common things you do in a dungeon.

  • 👁 Search for Traps — Roll Mind + d12. Finds floor traps, tresher traps, door traps, and hidden doors nearby. Lights up when a trap has been found so you can disarm it.
  • ⚔ Attack — Attack the nearest monster in weapon range (costs 1 AE). Shows as ✊ when no weapon is equipped (unarmed punch). Lights up gold when a valid target is adjacent.
  • 💰 Take All — Pick up every key, tresher, item, potion, and spell on your current square in one tap. Lights up gold when there is loot to grab.
  • 🚪 Open Door — Open an unlocked closed door adjacent to you. Lights up gold when a door can be opened.
  • 🔑 Use Key — Unlock a locked door if you are carrying the correct key. Lights up gold when a matching key is in your inventory.
  • ⌐¬ Pick Lock / Disarm Trap — Attempt to pick a locked door (Mind + d12 vs DC) or disarm a found trap. Lights up gold when either action is available.

Turns and Combat

The game is turn-based. The status bar at the bottom shows whether it is Your Turn or Monster Turn.

Your Turn

  • You start each turn with your full AE (5 or 4 depending on species).
  • Every action — moving, attacking, drinking a potion — costs 1 AE (diagonal moves cost 2).
  • When your AE hits 0 it is automatically the monsters' turn. You can also click End Turn early.

Attacking

  • Hit roll: d12 + weapon bonus + Stamina vs monster AC. Roll equal to or above the monster's AC to hit.
  • Damage: d(12 ÷ weapon tier) + Strength. Better weapons roll bigger dice. Without a weapon you punch for 1 + Strength ÷ 2 (min 1) but take a −1 to-hit penalty.
  • You can equip up to 2 hands worth of weapons from your inventory. Weapons have a range stat — a range above 1 lets you hit from further away.
  • Combo bonus: Hit the same monster repeatedly and each consecutive strike adds up to +4 to your hit roll, making sustained pressure increasingly accurate.

Boost Dice

  • Rolling the maximum on any to-hit die triggers a boost die added to the roll — and that boost die can itself chain if it also rolls max.
  • Chain: d12 max → +1d6 → d6 max → +1d4 → d4 max → +1d3 → d3 max → +1d2 → d2 max → +1d2 (repeats).
  • Applies to all PC attacks, monster attacks, and spell to-hit rolls.

Boost Attack (Fighter only)

  • Fighters can choose Boost Attack as their first action of the turn instead of a normal attack.
  • Cost: all remaining AE for that turn — the turn ends immediately after the attack.
  • Bonus to hit: +1d3 (lower levels), increasing to +1d6 at higher levels.
  • Trade-off: your AC is reduced by −4 until your next turn, leaving you vulnerable to counter-attacks.

Defending

  • Monster hit roll: d12 + monster bonus vs your AC + Stamina.
  • Your base AC is 10. Equipping armor treshers and armor items raises it.
  • Use the Defend action (costs 1 AE) to add +2 AC until your next turn. You can defend multiple times — each use stacks, up to your Number of Defends (NOD) stat.
  • When HP reaches 0 it's game over — "You have fallen. Your adventure ends here."

Number of Attacks & Defends

  • NOA (Number of Attacks) — how many attacks you can make per turn. Most PCs start at 1; this can be raised at the Tavern.
  • NOD (Number of Defends) — how many times you can Defend per turn. Extra defends stack their +2 AC bonus, creating a powerful defensive stance.

Monster behaviour

  • Monsters detect you when you are within 5 squares and they have line of sight.
  • Some monsters flee when they drop below a certain HP threshold.
  • Monsters with the roam flag wander the dungeon even when you are far away.
  • Some monsters call reinforcements — when they spot you, nearby allies join the fight.
  • Killing a monster can drop treshers and keys — and earns you SP.

Leveling Up & the Tavern

Your PC's level is calculated automatically from your stats — the stronger your Strength, Stamina, Mind, and Magic Power, the higher your level. Higher levels increase the Boost Attack bonus and may unlock other bonuses.

Spending SP at the Tavern

Between runs you can visit the Tavern on the Dashboard to spend your accumulated SP on permanent stat upgrades for your PC:

  • Strength — deals more damage
  • Stamina — improves attack rolls and AC when defending
  • Mind — better spell accuracy, trap detection, and magic resistance
  • Magic Power — more MP for spellcasting
  • Agility — improves ranged to-hit and Thieph sneak attacks
  • Number of Attacks (NOA) — attack more times per turn
  • Number of Defends (NOD) — defend more times per turn

Each upgrade costs 1 SP and permanently improves that stat by 1.

The Info Panel

The side panel holds your Combat Log, a Place Inventory panel showing what is around you, and your Inventory panel.

Combat Log

  • A scrolling log of every attack, spell, trap, and event this run — great for reviewing what just happened.

Nearby

  • Lists nearby monsters, then what you can pick up on your current square, then nearby doors, text, and other objects.
  • Doors still show their state (open, closed, locked) and action buttons — Open Door, Use Key, Pick Lock.
  • Has a Search for Traps button. Thieph and Shorties can search twice per turn; other classes once. If a trap is found a Disarm Trap button appears.
  • Pickup items show their own Take button, plus a Take All button for the whole square.
  • Shows Defend and Boost Attack (Fighter only) buttons for spending AE defensively or offensively mid-turn.

Inventory

  • Lists all keys and tresher items you are carrying.
  • Weapons have Equip / Unequip buttons. You have 2 hand slots; two-handed weapons use both.
  • Armor equips to your body slot and raises your AC.
  • Rings and necklaces equip to their own slots and provide passive bonuses.
  • Magic gems equip and add to your Magic Power.
  • Potions show a Drink button (costs 1 AE, cannot be used on monster turns). Some potions have a lasting effect that ticks over multiple turns.
  • Spells show a Cast (1 AE) button that lights up when a valid target is in range and you have enough MP.

Doors

  • Open — Walk straight through.
  • Closed — Use the 🚪 button or click Open Door in the Nearby panel.
  • Locked — Find the matching key (🔑) or pick the lock (⌐¬) with a Mind + d12 roll vs the DC.
  • Trapped — Search (👁) to detect the trap, then disarm it before it triggers on you or costs you HP.
  • Hidden — Looks exactly like a solid wall until you Search and roll Mind + d12 ≥ the Find DC (5–20). Once found it appears as a normal door you can open.

Treshers and Loot

Treshers are containers found on the dungeon floor or dropped by monsters. They can hold:

  • Coin (gold, silver, copper, zinc)
  • Weapons — equip from Inventory; have a to-hit bonus, damage tier, range, and optionally two-handed
  • Armor — equip from Inventory; raises your AC
  • Rings and necklaces — passive effect items
  • Magic gems — equip to boost Magic Power
  • Potions — drink healing potions (1 AE) to recover HP; lasting potions tick over turns
  • Spells — cast from Inventory tab (see Magic below)

Some treshers are trapped. Always search before grabbing a pile of loot in a suspicious spot.

Traps

Traps are hidden hazards on the floor, inside treshers, on doors, or behind hidden passages. When triggered they can cause:

  • HP damage — the most common trap effect
  • Stamina drain — lowers your attack rolls and AC
  • Mind drain — weakens spells and lock-picking
  • AE drain — steals action points from your current turn
  • Range of Sight reduction — narrows how far you can see

Use Search (👁) to detect traps. On success a Disarm Trap button appears — use it before triggering the trap. Lock-picking a trapped door can also set off the trap if your pick roll fails.

Magic

If your PC has spells in their inventory (inside a tresher), those spells appear in the Inventory tab with a Cast (1 AE) button.

Casting a Spell

  • Each spell cast costs 1 AE. It also spends the spell's Magic Cost from your MP pool.
  • You must have a live monster within the spell's range to cast it.
  • Spell hit roll: 1d12 + Mind + class modifier vs the spell's Target Number + monster's Magic Resistance. Roll equal to or above the total to succeed.
  • On a success the spell applies its effect (e.g. HP damage, stat reduction).
  • On a failure the spell fizzles — AE is still spent.
  • Class penalties: Fighters take −1 on spell cast rolls. Thiephs have no casting penalty.

Magic Resistance

  • PC Magic Resistance: Mind ÷ 2 (rounded down). Monsters must overcome this when they cast spells at you.
  • Monster Magic Resistance: set by the dungeon designer per monster. Adds directly to the DC of your spell roll.

Learning Spells

Spells can be found on the dungeon floor (or inside treshers). When you step on one, use Take All to collect it. Collected spells appear in the Inventory tab with a Learn button.

  • SP Cost — Each spell has an SP cost. You must have at least that many Skill Points to attempt learning it.
  • Min LTSP — Some spells also require a minimum Learn-Test SP threshold (shown as Min LTSP). Your total SP must meet this value before the Learn button activates.
  • GP Cost — Some spells require gold (GP) from your inventory in addition to SP. The cost is shown next to the spell name (e.g. 10 GP). Gold is drawn from whatever treshers you are currently carrying — if you can't cover the cost, the Learn button stays locked.
  • Learn roll: 1d12 + Mind ÷ 2 + class modifier vs the spell's Target Number. Fighters take −2; Thiephs take −1. SP and GP are only spent on a successful roll.
  • On a failed roll nothing is spent — you can try again next time you visit the spell.

Tips

  • Mage and Healer classes start with higher Magic Power, making them better spellcasters.
  • Stock up on gold before tackling dungeons with high-level spells — GP costs can be steep.
  • A higher Mind stat improves both your spell accuracy and your resistance to enemy magic.
  • Spells can hit from range — useful when you want to avoid melee.

Skill Points and the Exit

SP (Skill Points) are earned by killing monsters and completing dungeons. They appear in the status bar at the bottom and carry over to your PC permanently.

Spend SP at the Tavern (on the Dashboard) to permanently raise one of your PC's stats by 1 per SP — growing stronger between runs.

When you step onto the Exit square a prompt appears — confirm to leave the dungeon and collect any SP reward, or stay to keep exploring. Some exits use animated transitions; reach the top of the stairs to win.

Build Your Own Dungeons & Share Them

Love the game? You can design your own dungeons — draw them by hand in the editor, import a layout from a map generator, place a start point and exit, then stock the dungeon with monsters, treshers, keys, text, traps, portals, obstacles, and hidden passages. You can also use the Generate tool to fill an imported layout with themed content.

  • Keep a dungeon private for just you.
  • Share with specific friends from your friends list.
  • Go public so any player can start a run.
  • Import a map first, then use Generate to add monsters, loot, obstacles, and notes without changing the layout.

To unlock the Creator tools you need Creator access. Just reach out and apply — tell us a bit about what you want to make and we will get you set up. Once approved the Create link appears in the navigation bar.
